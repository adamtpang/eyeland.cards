# Eater of Worlds

Source: https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Eater_of_Worlds
Fetched: 2026-08-20T07:58:52.229Z

---

The Eater of Worlds is a pre-Hardmode Corruption-themed worm boss. It is not a single target, but a string of segments with individual health. When any inner body segment is destroyed, it splits into multiple smaller worms, each with their own head and tail. Each section of the Eater of Worlds must be killed in order to defeat the entire boss. The Eater of Worlds, like all enemies that pass through blocks, is immune to lava.
Fighting the Eater of Worlds is the only way to obtain Shadow Scales, and it is one of the only ways to obtain usable quantities of Demonite Ore. Individual segments drop some of these when killed, with a larger amount of them being dropped upon defeating the entire boss.
The Eater of Worlds is the Corruption counterpart to The Crimson's Brain of Cthulhu. The Hardmode mechanical boss equivalent to the Eater of Worlds is The Destroyer.
While the Eater of Worlds is alive, its exclusive music Eater of Worlds will play. When Otherworldly music is enabled, the track Boss 1 (Otherworldly) will play instead.


== Summoning and spawning ==
The Eater of Worlds spawns upon destroying every third Shadow Orb in a Corruption world.
It can also be summoned manually by using a Worm Food in The Corruption or Underground Corruption, at any time.


== Segments ==
The Eater of Worlds consists of 67 / 72 segments in total.


== Behavior ==
The Eater of Worlds initially spawns with all of its segments coiled in place, and then gradually uncoils while releasing visual purple particles from its body. Its segments are invisible before moving from their spawning position, but can still take damage in this state.
The Eater of worlds attacks the player by lunging towards the player in attempt to deal contact damage. While midair, it gradually becomes affected by gravity and is eventually forced to fall down, needing to "burrow" through solid blocks (including platforms and Planter Boxes, as well as top portion of furniture that can be stood on, such as Tables) or liquids before it can lunge towards the player again. While burrowing, it gradually builds up speed in direction of the player, up to a limit. Upon burrowing out of tiles at full speed, it can reach a maximum height of approximately 31 tiles before being forced to fall down.
The Eater of Worlds consists of a string of body and tail segments that are connected to a head segment, and follow movement of the head segment. However, unlike other worm enemies, the Eater of Worlds' segments do not share health. Instead, they each have their own health bar and all need to be killed separately to fully defeat the boss. Killing a segment can cause its adjacent segments to convert into a different segment type, and result in the boss splitting into multiple smaller worms that behave similarly to the original boss. The details of this mechanic are explained below:

Lone segments that are no longer connected to any other segment will instantly die, and drop their loot as normal.
Because of this, the smallest possible worm is one that is made of two segments in total: a head segment and a tail segment.
When a tail segment is killed, its adjacent body segment will be converted to a new tail segment.
When a head segment is killed, its adjacent body segment will be converted to a new head segment.
When a body segment is killed, its adjacent body segments will be converted to either a new tail segment or a new head segment, depending on which segment type the newly split worm would be missing.
The newly created head segments lose all of their momentum and immediately become affected by gravity, needing to fall down and burrow before they can attack the player.
If the head segment of one of the worms is outside of a 125×125-tile rectangle centered on the player, that worm becomes able to freely fly through the air similar to a Wyvern and is no longer affected by gravity. The worm will resume its normal behavior upon entering this rectangle again.
If the player dies or is in neither of the Corruption or Crimson biomes, all of the worms begin moving downwards and eventually despawn when they are in the bottom half of the Cavern layer, even if they are still on-screen. This behavior can be canceled if the player enters one of the Evil biomes before they despawn, causing them to target the player again.
Like most enemies in the game, the worms can also despawn if they are consistently off-screen for 12.5 seconds. The worms may despawn separately, which will still reduce total health of the boss.


=== Expert Mode ===
In Expert Mode, the Eater of Worlds has 5 extra body segments, all of its segments are 20% larger in size, and have 2 extra defense. It also has 20% increased maximum speed, and accelerates over twice as fast.
The increased speed allows it to reach a maximum height of approximately 44 tiles when burrowing out of tiles at full speed, before falling down.
The Eater of Worlds will randomly shoot Vile Spit from its head and body segments, as long as the segments have an unobstructed line of sight to the player. The Vile Spit projectiles can be destroyed in a single hit from any attack or projectile.
Body segments have a 1/900 (0.11%) chance per tick (average of once every 15 seconds) to shoot Vile Spit. They also can only shoot when they are above surface and are not in front of any background walls.
Head segments shoot more often as their individual health decreases. The chance for them to shoot Vile Spit gradually increases from 1/390 (0.26%) per tick (average of once every 6.5 seconds) at full health to 1/90 (1.11%) per tick (average of once every 1.5 seconds) just before dying.

== Notes ==
Trying to summon the Eater of Worlds with Worm Food while another is still alive will not work and the item will not be consumed.
Note that actually defeating the Eater of Worlds is not completely necessary to progress, as a certain amount of segments may drop the needed amount of materials.
However, defeating the Eater of Worlds is the only way to gain access to the Tavernkeep and Meteorites.
The different segments of the Eater of Worlds are not equivalent in stats: the head segment will deal significantly more damage than the body or tail, but also has the least defense.
The reverse is true for its tail; it has more defense than the body or head, but also deals the least damage.
The Eater of Worlds only takes 65% of the usual damage from the fire projectiles of the Molotov Cocktail and Obsidian Swordfish, and the projectiles will pierce 1 less target.
When the Skyblock tile threshold is reached, Eater of Worlds gains the ability to freely fly through the air at all times regardless of its distance to the player, without being affected by gravity or needing to burrow through tiles.
When the For the Worthy seed is active, Eater of Worlds receives the following changes:
All segments have 2 extra defense.
Head segments are 35% larger, while body and head segments are 40% larger.
This stacks multiplicatively with the Expert Mode size increase.
Maximum speed is increased by 40% compared to Classic Mode and by 33.33% compared to Expert Mode.
In Expert Mode, This allows it to reach a maximum height of approximately 77 tiles when burrowing out of tiles at full speed, before falling down.
Acceleration is increased by 71.4% compared to Classic Mode and by 33.33% compared to Expert Mode.
In Expert Mode, The chance for body segments to shoot Vile Spit is increased to 1/450 (0.22%) per tick (average of once every 7.5 seconds). They can also shoot Vile Spit at any height, but still cannot shoot when in front of background walls.
Additionally, the Vile Spit can no longer be destroyed while above surface, and has 42.9% increased velocity at all times.
Killing a head segment spawns a Big Eater of Souls.
While Eater of Worlds is alive, all Eater of souls gain 50% increased health and can shoot Vile Spit at the player at a rate of once per second. The Vile Spit fired by them is identical to the one fired by Eater of Worlds, and receives the same boosts mentioned above. In Expert Mode, they also gain 2 extra defense but do not benefit from the Hardmode stat scaling.
The Vile Spit does not get increased contact damage in Legendary Mode compared to Master Mode, despite technically being an enemy.
When the Celebration Mk 10 seed is active and the For the Worthy seed is inactive, Eater of Worlds' segments are 50% smaller.
This stacks multiplicatively with the Expert Mode size increase.
 The conditions of whether Dungeon Bricks and Dungeon Brick Walls (in a set of bricks and all three variations of walls with matching color. The color of both are chosen randomly) can be dropped is checked upon entering the world, or occasionally during gameplay. If no Dungeon Bricks and no Dungeon Brick Walls of any kind were found (i.e. no walls with ID 7, 8, 9, 94, 95, 96, 97, 98 nor 99 and no tiles with ID 41, 43, nor 44), Eater of Worlds will drop Dungeon building materials upon its defeat and reset the drop condition (i.e. it cannot drop them again until another check is performed and passed).

== Tips ==

If the player manages to quickly find the initial spawning location of Eater of Worlds, they can attack it with a piercing weapon while it is still in its coiled state, which will damage many of its segments at once and can potentially kill it in seconds.
Effective weapons include piercing or area-of-effect weapons, such as the Vilethorn, Spiky Balls, whips, and fast melee weapons, such as the Falcon Blade and the Terragrim.
In Classic Mode, Grenades are an effective weapon against the Eater. That is not true in higher difficulties, since the Eater gains resistance to explosives.
Like other worm-types, once the Eater's head passes a given point, every body segment must follow. This allows the player to stand next to the body, attacking every segment as it goes by. The player should be wary of the head looping back around.
Killing the head of the Eater of Worlds causes its speed to drastically decrease, almost to a stop.
If possible, try to aim for the head or tail of each section, as if they die first, the Eater of Worlds will not split. Note that the head is far more vulnerable.
If fighting in a chasm, be wary of getting surrounded, which can lead to death very quickly, especially in Expert or Master Mode. A quick way to escape is to have a Bed to teleport to (using Recall Potions or a Magic or Ice Mirror) above the battle area.
It may be preferable to hollow out an area in the Corruption in advance, in order to avoid this situation.
Farming the Eater of Worlds is particularly profitable, being one of the most effective ways of making money prior to Hardmode.
Converting the Demonite Ore to bars and selling its drops will yield an average of 19 Gold per kill.
If Vile Mushrooms run short, Vile Powder (for Worm Food) can be bought from the Dryad during a Blood Moon.
Since each segment can drop loot when killed, it can be a good source of Presents during Christmas, and Goodie Bags, Bloody Machetes, and Bladed Gloves during Halloween.
In Hardmode, it can provide other items as well:
It is a good way to farm for a Corruption Key, as every segment has a chance of dropping one.
In a Corrupt Snow biome, it can drop Amaroks, with each selling for a base value of 4 Gold.
In a Corrupt Ocean, it can drop Pirate Maps.
The Eater of Worlds will start to flee underground if the player leaves the Corruption biome, but will not immediately despawn. Players can utilize this mechanic to keep the Eater of Worlds away from themselves without despawning it by moving in and out of the Corruption, watching for the boss's icon on the minimap to make sure it does not leave.
Using a flail will block any Vile Spit from hitting the player when ⚒ Use / Attack is held down. Alternatively, a whip would also work for it can sweep a large area clean of Vile Spit in a single whip.


== Trivia ==

The Eater of Worlds and Skeletron are the only bosses that do not have their own unique AI.
Skeletron only shares its AI with the Dungeon Guardian.
The Eater of Worlds might spawn outside of map boundaries on rare occasions when breaking Shadow Orbs.
The Eater of Worlds is sometimes abbreviated as "EoW".
With five eyes on the body segments and three eyes on the head and tail segments each, the Eater of Worlds has 331 / 356 eyes in total.
The Eater of Worlds measures roughly 195 / 209 tiles.
Because the Compass and Depth Meter reveal each tile to be 2 feet in length, the Eater of Worlds would be about 130 yards (142.17 m) long in real life, or 139.33 yards (152.38 m) long in Expert Mode.
The Eater of Worlds has the highest health of all pre-Hardmode bosses.
Oddly enough, but most likely intentionally, it is not required to actually completely defeat the Eater of Worlds to get progression-locked items such as the Nightmare Pickaxe or Shadow armor, since each separate segment drops Shadow Scales.
Because the Eater of Worlds has no more unique drops than the mask, trophy, and the Eater's Bone in Classic Mode, this can prove beneficial for players who are not strong enough to fight it yet but want better gear.
However, in Expert Mode and above, defeating it does reward the player with the Worm Scarf.
The ability to drop progression-locked materials is a trait shared with Brain of Cthulhu, the Crimson counterpart to this boss.
 – According to the Terraria lore pages, the Destroyer is a simulacrum of Cthulhu's spine. Despite the Destroyer taking heavy influence from it, the Eater of Worlds has no connection to Cthulhu and is not mentioned to be a former body part, unlike other pre-mech forms.
According to Redigit, the Eater of Worlds is named Edward. This statement was most likely a joke and is not considered official lore.
The Steam Workshop page appears to show the Eater of Words with centipede-like legs rather than its usual worm-like depiction.
Contrary to its name, the Eater of Worlds does not "consume" or destroy any blocks.
According to the Angler, a severed piece of the Eater of Worlds fell into the water and mutated into a corrupt fish, creating the Eater of Plankton.
 The Bestiary entry for the Eater of Worlds: "Conceived from the bottomless malice of the Corruption, this mighty abyssal worm tunnels wildly to devour all in its path."
