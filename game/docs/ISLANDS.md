# Islands

## Direction

Islands are themed worlds/content chapters, not tiles created by playing cards. Each new island supplies new cards, creatures, bosses and a quest line, with later class/mechanic unlocks. Roughly ten levels per island is the first-arc planning rhythm. The first release may be only the home island, with later chapters added episodically.

## First-arc scaffold — hypotheses

| Chapter | Approximate levels | Narrative/mechanical purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1: home | 1–10 | Relationships, weak local power, resource economy, five-card learning, pirate crisis |
| 2: wider shore | 11–20 | First outside faction and a new card interaction |
| 3: deepening world | 21–30 | More intentional team roles/ally relationships |
| 4: contested domain | 31–40 | Faction pressure and a meaningful allegiance question |
| 5: arc climax | 41–50 | Pay off the arc's threat and reveal the next power horizon |

Only the weak home and wider progression are strong direction. Names, biomes, level boundaries and the assistant's proposed order for islands 2–5 remain placeholders. There are four elements and five islands: do not assume a one-to-one assignment. Mixed-element islands and domains can exist.

## Content template

For each authored island define its emotional promise, residents, resource, visible threat, relationship anchor, encounter set, new card decision, recipe teacher, rest/social place, traversal tease, boss counter opportunity, departure condition and one unanswered lore question. Reuse systems; introduce at most one major new teaching burden at a time as a working production rule.
