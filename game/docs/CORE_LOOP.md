# Core loop

## Direction

Choose a goal → travel/explore → encounter creatures or opponents → play a Hearthstone-style battle → earn cards/resources → change a deck or craft toward a desired card → recover → push farther. New power exposes new islands, opponents, quests, classes and combinations; those places supply the next meaningful improvement.

Collection is persistent across adventures. Deck and party loadouts should express a play style and prepare for a dungeon. Optional friends, tavern duels and trading fit between adventures rather than replacing the adventure loop.

## Session hypothesis

A satisfying 30–60 minute session includes a clear goal, travel, a few fights, a rest, and one meaningful choice or breakthrough. Adam endorsed this session outline; exact durations and reward pacing are untested. Avoid repetitive errands whose only purpose is filling an XP bar.

## First-hour proposal

1. Meet someone who makes home matter; see its valuable resource.
2. Choose a simple class/element identity and receive a legendary plus four supporting cards.
3. Win a readable encounter and understand how minions, spells, cost and attacks work.
4. Receive a creature card/resource; make an intentional deck change.
5. Find a rest point, ally possibility and hint of the wider elemental world.
6. Witness the pirate threat and leave with a personal reason to grow.

This is a proposed ordering, not six implemented quests. The immediate slice proves only a small walk → encounter → battle → reward → home interaction. A complete first island comes after this.

## Validation

Observe whether a new player can enter battle, explain their starter, make legal plays, notice the reward and want another encounter without coaching. Distinguish no opportunity to draw a card from failure to understand it. Record confusion, session length and enjoyment; automated simulations establish rule execution, not these outcomes.
