# Eyeland: an adventure that builds your deck

Adam's direction, clarified 2026-09-11: collectible creature cards with visible personalities, Pokemon-style creature collection, resources and crafting, persistent RPG progression, and multiplayer questing inspired by Wizard101.

**Explore islands, earn the creatures you defeat, craft new possibilities, and take your growing spellbook on adventures with friends.**

The current duel uses familiar mana/pips, creatures, spells, attack/health and targeting. The main differentiation should come from how the world, collection and party adventure connect. A different skin alone does not establish that difference.

| Pillar | Eyeland direction | Current prototype |
|---|---|---|
| Creature collection | Find creatures in their habitats; their cards become memories and tools from the adventure. | Defeating each camp awards its creature card. No separate capture action yet. |
| Resources and crafting | Encounters and exploration supply ingredients for specific cards, equipment or recipes. | Ember shards awarded and saved; recipes not built. |
| RPG progression | A persistent character, quests and growing build options unlock harder islands and encounters. | Collection and four-camp progress persist; character XP, levels, equipment and quest system not built. |
| Cooperative questing | Friends explore and take on shared encounters with complementary decks and roles. | Solo only; party combat and networking not built. |
| Card identity | Recognizable illustrated creatures in collection, hand and battle, with readable rules and stats. | Eight Ember Reach creature portraits and card inspection added in this visual pass. |

## Next experiments, not implementation promises

1. One meaningful crafting recipe: spend encounter resources on a card that opens a new tactic. Test whether players choose a destination because they want its ingredient.
2. One quest with a visible reward and a persistent character unlock. Avoid adding several currencies or progression bars before testing this choice.
3. One two-player cooperative encounter. Prove shared turns, targeting and rewards before a large shared world.

Preserve the confirmed victory-card reward. If capture later becomes a separate decision, decide its relationship to guaranteed victory rewards explicitly; do not silently add random capture failure. Preserve Common, Rare, Epic and Legendary. Character growth should introduce choices as well as strength, so early creatures can remain useful.

The first playtest question remains: does earning the Wolf lead to a new tactic in the next fight? See ELAN-LEE-LESSONS.md and PLAY-EMBER-REACH.md. These are proposed design experiments, not validated fun or completed multiplayer systems.
